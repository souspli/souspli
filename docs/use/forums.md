# Forums and groups

A forum in Souspli is not a place. Nobody hosts it, nobody can take it down, and
there is no admin panel. It is a handful of letter types that point at each other:

| A forum needs | In Souspli it is |
|---|---|
| a place | a **Group** letter — a roster with roles |
| a post | any letter tagged *in* that group |
| a thread | **Comment** letters that reply to a post, and to each other |
| a vote | a **Vote** letter: one key, for or against one letter |
| joining | a **Request to join** letter, answered by a new roster |
| moderation | an **Attestation** by someone the roster calls a moderator |
| whose opinion counts | [your tribe](people.md) — the keys *you* vouched for |

Every one of those is an ordinary signed letter that travels like any other. A forum
"exists" wherever people hold its letters.

## Start one

1. **New → Group.** Give it a name and a purpose. Add members: each is a key, a
   role and a name. Give yourself a role; name at least one **moderator** if you want
   moderation. Publish.
2. **Share…** it — and to make it a living forum, **Post to relays** on a relay you
   and your members have all added. A forum works on relays alone, because posts,
   comments and votes are small enough to travel whole.
3. Tell people which relay, and send them the group letter.

You are the group's **keeper**: the roster is your letter, and only you can publish
a new version of it.

## Read and post

**File → Forums…** lists every group you hold. Open one to see its posts, ranked.

- **Write a post** starts a draft tagged into the group (a *News article* by
  default). Publish it, then post it to the relay.
- Open a post to read its thread. **Comment** replies to the post or to any comment;
  threads nest as deep as they go.
- **▲ ▼** casts a vote. A vote is signed immediately, without a confirmation dialog,
  because you pressed a control in Souspli itself on something already on your
  screen.

Anyone can tag a letter into any group — being *in* a forum is the author's claim,
and the roster never agreed to it. What keeps that from being chaos is the next
section.

## Ranking: "47 votes, 6 from your tribe"

Votes are free to manufacture. A thousand throwaway keys cost nothing, so a raw
count proves nothing. What cannot be faked is a path that starts at *your* key: the
people you have [vouched for](people.md), and the people they vouched for.

So every score shows **both numbers, side by side**, and listings sort by tribe
score first, then raw score, then recency. Two people reading the same forum — the
identical letters — see a different order, because each one's library weights the
same votes by whose vouches reach the voter. That is the intended behaviour, not a
bug: there is no global ranking for anyone to game.

> **If you have vouched for nobody**, every tribe score is zero and the order falls
> back to raw popularity — the forgeable kind. The forum window tells you this in
> words. Vouch for someone you actually know and the order starts meaning something.

A vote is about a **letter**. A vouch is about a **person**. They are kept apart on
purpose: if agreeing with someone pulled them into your trust graph, anyone could
buy their way in with agreeable posts.

## Joining

Press **Request to join** in a forum. That publishes a small letter saying you would
like to be on the roster. It admits nobody: *asking is not joining*.

The keeper sees pending requests under **Asking to join**. To admit someone, the
keeper opens the group, presses **New version…**, adds them to the roster and
publishes. The request stops being pending the moment a roster names its author —
nobody marks it accepted. The roster *is* the answer.

Someone written out in version 3 is no longer a member, whatever version 2 said:
membership is always read from the latest version you hold.

## Moderation

Nobody can delete anyone's signed letter, so moderation can only be advice. In
Souspli it is advice you can audit:

- A verdict is an attestation — **hide** or **endorse** — that names the post and
  the forum, signed by a key the roster you hold calls a moderator.
- A hidden post is **folded, never dropped**. The row says who hid it and why, with
  **Show anyway** beside it. Disagreeing has to stay possible; that is the
  difference between a forum and a memory hole.
- An endorsed post carries an *endorsed* badge.
- A moderator's reach is exactly the roster *you* hold. Hold no copy of the group
  and nobody moderates anything for you; hold a later version that drops them and
  their reach ends there.
- A verdict names its forum, so moderating one group gives no power in another.

**If the roster you hold names you a moderator**, each unjudged post in the forum
window carries **Hide…** and **Endorse…**. Pressing one does not issue a verdict: it
starts a draft that already names the post, the forum and your ruling. Say why, press
**Publish**, confirm — and then **Post to relays**, because a verdict is a letter
like any other and reaches other readers the same way.

## Your main feed stays readable

A busy forum must not bury a memo from your boss. In the main feed a vote never gets
its own row, and a reply folds into the post it answers, which shows
reply and vote counts instead. A reply to something you do not hold still shows
on its own, because hiding it would hide your only copy.
