Full text, no advertising, a recirculation module built from her last forty articles.

Four paragraphs, then an offer priced by his country and his visit count.

A revised headline, a corrected figure, different advertising. No notice that anything moved.

There is no artefact to keep, to compare, or to disagree with — only a rendering, and it is gone when the tab closes.

REACH

Anatomy of a letter

THE RIVERSIDE LEDGER · 14 MAY 2026

Flood defences approved after eight-year delay

Signed by The Riverside Ledger · sha256 4f9c…21ab
  • One file of HTML. Text, images, styling and behaviour, all inside it.
  • Readable as source by a person, and openable by any browser for as long as browsers exist.
  • Signed by its author. The hash is the identity, so any edit makes a different document.
  • Run in a sealed container: rich and interactive, with no way out to the network.

Five guarantees

1

Self-contained

No external fetches. What you received is the whole of it.

2

Human-readable

Open technologies, plain markup, no build artefact to decode.

3

Immutable

Identified by content. A correction is a new letter, not a silent edit.

4

Attributable

Signed by the sender, so provenance travels with the file.

5

Sealed

Interactive, but with no exit. It cannot report on its reader.

Archiving is then a copy operation, and the copy is a complete one.

The newspaper

01

The publisher composes

The edition is written as one self-contained file, advertising included.

02

The publisher signs

A signature and a content hash fix the edition. Nothing in it can move afterwards.

03

A reader fetches it

From the publisher, or from anyone who already has it. The bytes are identical.

04

The reader keeps it

Read it now or in a decade, shelve it, or hand it to a friend.

The advertisement inside is the one everyone else received — which is what an advertisement used to be.

The schema rides inside the letter, so a vendor's agent can reject a withdrawn SKU on its own machine and reply with a memorandum — itself a letter.
